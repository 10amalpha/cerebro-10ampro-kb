There is No Case for A Fed Rate Cut Right Now #fed #bonds #equities #dollar #emergingmarkets
Today's US March inflation release was not awful (+0.4% headline and core); it was just not weak enough to move the Fed closer to a new rate-cutting cycle. I have been on vacation in Spain - which btw seems very cheap to a US resident - but now that I am back we seem further from a Fed cut than ever. My most recent post (18/3) argued for two Fed rate cuts in 2024, one in July and one after the election, most likely in December. I do not expect the Fed to start a rate-cutting cycle just before the election (in Sept.) nor just after (in Nov.). I still see this as a viable overall scenario.

But, first, the data. Recent inflation reports, added to by the March data have been far too resilient for a year when many thought the Fed would cut rates 6-7 times. Take a look at just some of the details:
- Headline inflation is stuck above 3% (3.5% in March); core inflation is falling only very slowly (+3.8%);
- The 'short-term' inflation rates are rebounding. On a 3-month per annum basis, headline inflation was 4.4% in March, the highest since Sept. 2023; on the same basis, core inflation rose to 4.8% in March, the highest since last May. These are bad numbers;
- The more medium rates are behaving a bit better. Six-month headline inflation is 3.2% pa, up from recent lows of 3.0% in late-2023; on the same basis, however, core inflation has risen steadily from 3.0% last November to 4.0% in March;
- Just like my golf game, there is always something disappointing in these inflation reports. In March, it was a 1.1% jump in Energy prices with gasoline prices up 1.7%. With Brent now around $90/bbl, there may be more of this to come. Medical care prices rose 0.6%. Shelter prices were in line with the headline rate 0.4%. Food prices rose only 0.1% and used car prices fell sharply.

In sum, with the US economy continuing to defy expectations of a sharp slowdown and, in particular, the labor market still strong, there is absolutely NO case for a rate cut right now. My call of 2 cuts starting in July may be the best we can now hope for and even a cut as 'early' as July requires some much softer inflation data to get there. (Luckily, we have three more inflation reports before then.) There will be growing talk of no cuts in 2024 or even the next Fed move being another rate hike.

What does this do for EM equities? Not much, I fear, especially as today's report has lifted the USD again. However, it is worth noting that, as the case for multiple Fed rate cuts has faded over recent weeks, EM has been resilient; MSCI EM closed at a 2024 high last night, up 3.1% YTD in $. A 'more conventional' rally so far in 2024, led by EM Asia (+4.4%), with China less of a drag than in the past three years, has helped overall sentiment. Assuming I am right and that we eventually do get round to Fed rate cuts later this year, I still see a mildly positive outlook for EM equities in 2024.
