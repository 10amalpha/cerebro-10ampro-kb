The AI Trade Is Now a Power Trade

Last November, I argued that the AI boom would not be decided solely by models, chips, or software. It would be decided by who could secure the watts, concrete, copper, transformers, turbines, and transmission required to turn compute demand into operating data centers.

My contentious thesis, S&P 500 target of 8000 with a possibility of 8400 if all goes well in 2026. Yes I suggested the 4 year cycle was broken.

That thesis has moved from forecast to deployment.

America’s data-center power consumption is positioned to roughly double over the next five years, requiring on the order of 95–100 gigawatts of new capacity. Individual AI campuses may demand one to five gigawatts, loads once associated with industrial complexes or small cities. Cheap, reliable electricity is no longer a utility-side detail. It is a strategic asset.

The market’s most revealing signal may be that retired jet engines are being repurposed as stationary natural-gas turbines. ProEnergy is retrofitting CF6 aircraft-engine cores into roughly 48 MW power units for data centers; FTAI is converting CFM56 engines into 25 MW turbines. This is a tangible response to turbine delivery backlogs stretching toward 2029 and grid connections that can take years.

It recalls the pressure-pumping shortages of prior energy cycles: when the bottleneck becomes acute, capital does not wait for the textbook solution. It finds, adapts, and reprices available equipment. The shortage is not abstract. It is embodied in transformer lead times of up to four years, turbine backlogs, constrained skilled labor, and scarce interconnection rights.

Wall St. broader AI and data-center framework puts global investment through 2030 at roughly $5 trillion, with higher scenarios reaching $7 trillion. That spend must flow beyond chips into generation, transmission, cooling, substations, electrical construction, and secured power supply.

Now the era of Compute as an Asset Class is upon us. The Standardization and Securitization of AI Cap Ex finance.  Will provide the capital for the build out.

But the market phase is changing and evolving. The first phase was indiscriminate: buy anything AI-adjacent, tolerate weak balance sheets, and watch low-quality names become five-baggers while NVIDIA consolidated. That easy beta phase is largely over.

We are moving from early-cycle broadening to a mid-cycle quality rotation. The market now demands not just growth, but growth supported by durable earnings, pricing power, strong margins, credible delivery schedules, and free cash flow. Yes the winners have been identified.

The next winners will not be every company with “AI” in a presentation. They will be the owners of scarce capacity, and the operators that can execute against expectations without paying any price for growth. The theme remains powerful. The valuation discipline now matters just as much. To wit, S&P 500 target of 10,000 for 2027, is not out of the question.
